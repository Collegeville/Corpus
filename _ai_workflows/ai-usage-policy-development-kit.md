*As of this writing (2026). Institutional and course-level AI policies are still evolving —
revisit this page periodically rather than treating it as permanent guidance.*

## Definition

Rather than following someone else's blanket AI policy, this is a habit of writing your own:
answering five specific questions to produce an explicit, personal policy for how you'll use AI
tools in your work. This matters more, not less, as institutional policy stays vague — most
schools and workplaces don't have a specific policy for generative AI; use typically falls under
general honesty and misconduct rules instead, which tell you what's prohibited without telling
you how to use permitted AI assistance *well*. Writing your own policy is what closes that gap:
a forcing function for thinking carefully about how you use AI and how you make sure the result
is still genuinely yours, connected to the same authorship principles covered in
[About Corpus → AI as Collaborator]({{ '/about-corpus/ai-as-collaborator/' | relative_url }}).

## Learning Outcome

After using this technique, you should have an explicit, written personal policy — not just a
vague sense of "I try to be honest about it" — that you could actually hand to someone else and
follow yourself under pressure.

## Core Structure

Five questions, each demanding a specific, concrete answer rather than a general intention:

1. **Transparency.** How will you ensure transparency when using AI in your work? (Not "I'll be
   honest" — a specific practice: what gets disclosed, where, in what format.)
2. **Boundaries.** In what situations will you avoid using AI tools entirely? (Not "when it
   feels wrong" — specific situations you can recognize in the moment.)
3. **Verification.** How will you verify the accuracy and fairness of AI-generated output?
   (A specific check you'll actually perform, not just "I'll double-check it.")
4. **Privacy.** What privacy and data protection practices will you follow when using AI? (What
   you will and won't paste into a tool, and why.)
5. **Disclosure.** How will you disclose AI involvement in your deliverables? (The actual
   language or format you'll use, not just "I'll mention it.")

## Worked Example

A sample answer to question 1 (transparency), specific enough to actually follow: "I'll keep a
running note of every AI-assisted step in a document alongside my draft — what I asked, what I
got back, and what I changed before it went into the final version — so I could reconstruct my
process for anyone who asked, not just summarize it from memory afterward." That's concrete
enough to actually do, unlike "I'll be transparent about it," which isn't a practice at all.

## Common Pitfalls

- Answering all five questions with some version of "I'll be honest / careful / responsible" —
  none of that is a policy, it's a value statement with no practice attached.
- Setting boundaries so vague they'd never actually stop you in the moment ("I won't overuse
  it").
- Treating verification as a one-time check rather than a repeatable practice you'd apply to
  every piece of AI-assisted work.
- Writing the policy once and never revisiting it as tools, coursework, or your own judgment
  change.

## Checklist

- [ ] Transparency practice is a specific, repeatable action, not a value statement
- [ ] At least one concrete situation is named where AI won't be used at all
- [ ] Verification has an actual method attached, not just an intention
- [ ] Privacy practice specifies what will and won't be shared with a tool
- [ ] Disclosure has a real format or phrasing, ready to actually use
