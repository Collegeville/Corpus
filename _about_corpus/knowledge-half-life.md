summary: The rationale for why some skills are timeless and others need periodic refresh.

Every piece of knowledge has a half-life — some span decades, some span months — and the entire
architecture of this site exists because of that fact. A course, or an archive, that treats
"how to structure a technical argument" and "the current best prompting pattern for a specific
tool" as the same kind of content ends up doing a disservice to both: the durable material gets
buried under things that will be outdated within a year, and the timely material gets treated
with more permanence than it deserves.

So Corpus is split in two by half-life. **Human Skills** hold the material with a long
half-life — techniques that don't meaningfully change even as the tools around them do. The
material with a short half-life — genuinely useful right now, explicitly labeled as needing
periodic revisiting rather than presented as settled fact — comes in two forms: **AI-Assisted
Workflows**, run by a human with an AI tool's help, and **Agent Skills**, downloadable files an
agent runs directly.

This is philosophy, not technique — the *why* behind the site's whole structure. The applied,
practical version of the same idea — a set of questions for sizing up any specific thing you're
about to spend time learning, right now, regardless of what it is — lives as its own page:
[Knowledge Half-Life]({{ '/human-skills/knowledge-half-life/' | relative_url }}) in Human Skills. Read this page for the
reasoning behind the split; read that one when you're actually trying to decide how much to
invest in learning something new.
