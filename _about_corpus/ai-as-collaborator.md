title: AI as Collaborator
summary: The course's stance on generative AI tools and authorship.

---

CS373's stance on generative AI tools has never been prohibition. Since the earliest capable
tools became available, the course embraced them as a genuine part of how research, writing, and
presenting get done — while holding a hard line on what that use has to mean for authorship.

Three guiding principles carry that line:

1. **Identify what's yours as author, and what came from somewhere else.** Using an AI tool
   doesn't blur this distinction; it makes it more important to keep track of than ever.
2. **Find and cite sources for content obtained from others.** This rule doesn't change because
   the "somewhere else" is now sometimes a chat window instead of a journal article.
3. **Acknowledge the use of generative AI tools, but don't cite them as a source.** An AI tool
   isn't a reference the way a paper or an expert is — it's closer to a very capable assistant,
   and gets acknowledged as one.

The class policy built from those principles is short enough to state directly: embrace
generative AI tools; take full responsibility for the content of your own papers and
presentations regardless of how they were produced; use these tools to accelerate learning
content, never to replace learning it.

That last clause is the one that actually gets enforced, and it's enforced through
[Level of Expertise Dialogue]({{ '/human-skills/level-of-expertise-dialogue/' | relative_url }}) rather than through
policing what tools a student used. An unscripted conversation about your own work is a poor
place to hide behind something you don't actually understand — which means there's no advantage
to using AI tools to shortcut real understanding, and no need to treat their use as something to
hide. The incentive structure does the work that a stricter usage policy would otherwise have to.

None of this stays fixed. Tools change, and so does the shared sense of what's appropriate. What
should hold steady is the underlying commitment: you take responsibility for what you submit,
you're honest about how you produced it, and you can speak to it yourself when asked. The AI
Skill [AI Usage Policy Development Kit]({{ '/ai-skills/ai-usage-policy-development-kit/' | relative_url }}) is the
practical exercise built around that commitment — writing your own explicit policy, specific
enough to actually follow, rather than a general intention to "be responsible about it."
